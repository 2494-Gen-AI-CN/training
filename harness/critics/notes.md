# Notes-page critic — PASS criteria

You are judging `docs/weeks/<week>/<day>.html`. You are NOT the author. Read the page, the research brief (the ONLY allowed facts: its Claims, Notes depth, Sources, Glossary and Pitfalls), the template named in your prompt (structure gold standard; `harness/config.json` `template`, falling back to `harness/templates/day.exemplar.html`) and `harness/metrics.json` `notes`. Use Bash + Python to count, do not estimate: run `python harness/notes_check.py <page> --counts --phrases --stamps --citations --style --links` and quote its output. Return `pass=false` with specific critiques if ANY item fails.

The page is reference and study material (D-0040): a reader who studies it answers junior-developer interview questions on every subtopic of the day. Application is the demos' job; the page explains, shows, names the trade-off, and gives the words to say.

## Structure (must match the template)

1. `<meta name="tc-day" content="<id>">` present; `<title>` ends with the site title.
2. Topbar: brand, Home, Demos, Exercises links (relative path `../../index.html`; Demos = `config.demoRepo.url`, the public demo repo and the ONLY demo link on the site, D-0055; Exercises = the config repo URL).
3. `main.lesson2 > .reading2col` with `nav.toc`, `article.reading-col`, `aside.side`.
4. `.lede` has `h1`, `.meta` with "Week W · Day D", the date, the week theme, "~N min" where N == round(prose words / `readingWpm`) within ±1, and `span.verified` reading "Sources checked YYYY-MM-DD" (the brief's `verified:` date) followed by "· <lib> <version>" stamps that all exist in the brief's `version-stamps:` (python at least). The `.summary` names in one sentence what the page assumes from the previous day, what it teaches and what the reader can answer afterwards.
5. `section.step` count within `metrics.notes.stepsMin..stepsMax`; `data-step` values are 0..n-1 in order; each has a `.tag` and an `h2`.
   - 5b. Every TEACHING step (all but the last) has ≥ `h3PerStepMin` `h3` subsections and exactly `interviewPerStep` `div.callout.interview` as its LAST element, with `div.k` "Say it in an interview" and 1–3 `<p>` in spoken form: first person, no code, each sayable in 10–20 seconds, each answering one of the step's interview questions from the brief.
   - 5e. (D-0060) Immediately BEFORE that callout sits `<div class="recap"><div class="k">Takeaways</div><ul>…</ul></div>`: `recapItemsMin..recapItemsMax` items (`metrics.notes.prose`), each one plain line of ≤ `recapItemWordsMax` words that restates something the step taught (no new fact, no code block, no bold). A recap that merely repeats the h3 headings, or one the reader could not use to decide whether to reread the step, is a critique.
   - 5c. The LAST step's `.tag` is "Check yourself" with an `h2`, one intro `<p>`, and `checkYourselfMin..checkYourselfMax` `details.check` blocks, each `<summary>` a retrieval question and a `<p>` answer of 1–4 sentences that is correct against the brief. No h3, no interview callout, no citation requirement in this step.
   - 5d. Page total of `<pre>` code blocks ≥ `codeBlocksMin`; every block is runnable as shown (or a terminal transcript) and is followed IMMEDIATELY by its output: a `<pre class="out">` holding what it prints (preferred, D-0060) or a `<p>` that states it. A block followed by an `h3` or another block is a critique. `pre.out` is output, not code: it is not counted in `codeBlocksMin` and never carries a prompt character. The two `<pre>` inside a `.ix-task` (item 17) are a task's starter and its checks, not examples, and are not held to this.
6. `section.reading-sources` with ≥ `sourcesMin` `<li id="src-N">` items (N = 1..), each one `http` link plus a one-line reason, every URL taken from the research brief (Sources, Claims or Notes depth).
7. Aside: `.side-diagram` with `svg#diagram` + `.stage-caption`; `.side-cards` containing a quiz `.activity-card[data-modal="quiz-modal"]` and an optional deep-dive card, and NO demo card (D-0055: demos are disconnected from the site; a `.demo-card` or `data-demo` attribute anywhere is a FAIL); `.side-nav`.
8. Modals: `#quiz-modal` (with `section#quiz` + `.modal-next`), `#goals-modal` (`goalsMin..goalsMax` `.goals-list` items + "Today's exercise" link to the exercise README; NO demo list, D-0055), optional `#deep-modal`; no `#code-*` popups.
9. Script tags, in order: `theme.js` in head; at the bottom exactly `diagram.js, scrolly.js, modals.js, quiz.js, widgets.js, glossary.js` (no `survey.js` on any day, D-0061; no `codeviewer.js`, D-0055). No other scripts. No `FDE_`, `roster`, `progress.js`, `day-summary`, `auth.js`, `workers.dev`, `2463` anywhere.

## Content

10. Prose word count (`notes_check.py --counts`: text inside `.reading-col` excluding `.reading-sources`, code, buttons and citation markers) within `wordMin..wordMax` from `metrics.notes`. Every teaching step carries 450–900 of them.
    - 10b. READABILITY BAR (D-0060, `metrics.notes.prose`; `notes_check.py --style` counts it, you judge it). Inside the teaching steps: no paragraph over `paragraphWordsMax` prose words; no sentence over `sentenceWordsMax` words or with more than `codeSpansPerSentenceMax` inline `<code>` spans (interview callouts and check answers are exempt: they are spoken answers); at least `structuredBlocksMin` lists or tables in teaching steps on the page. Beyond the counts: a sentence that lists three or more parallel things ("how to X, how to Y, and how to Z"; a run of file modes, exception types or dict keys) is a `<ul>` or, when the items have two or more attributes each, a `<table>`; a comparison of options is a table, never a paragraph; a paragraph holds one idea. Quote the sentence and give the list or table it should be.
11. Every factual claim traces to the research brief (Claims, Notes depth, Pitfalls, Glossary). The author adds NOTHING to the brief. Flag invented numbers, versions, API names, defaults, limits, prices and behaviour claims the brief does not carry.
    - 11b. Citations: every teaching step has ≥ `citationsPerStepMin` `<sup class="cite"><a href="#src-N">N</a></sup>` markers; every marker resolves to an `li#src-N`; every sentence that states a version number, a model id, an API/class/function/flag name's behaviour, a default value, a limit or a price carries one, and the cited source actually supports that sentence.
12. Scope: the page teaches THIS day's coverage rows, each subtopic under an `h2` or an `h3`, in curriculum order, plus the adjacent concept the brief's Notes depth names for it. Nothing an anti-spoiler row defers to a later day is an `h2` or `h3` (one sentence that names where it lands is fine). The "Demos today" list names the plan's demos for the day in ladder order, by objective. The plan's Notes-step numbers are realigned by the run's Wire step from what the author built: do not fail on them.
    - 12b. DEPTH BAR (the reason the page exists). Pick three subtopics of the day. For each, pose one junior-interview question and the follow-up one level beyond (the brief's Notes depth lists them). PASS only if the page answers both with all five elements present somewhere in that subtopic's section: a plain explanation a graduate can follow, a runnable example with its output stated, a trade-off or limit, how to recognise it on sight in someone else's code, and a spoken answer in the interview callout. Quote the gap when one is missing.
    - 12c. Delivery references: zero hits of `metrics.notes.deliveryPhrases` in article prose, quiz strings or diagram strings (`notes_check.py --phrases`); no sentence that refers to the classroom, the session, the trainer or what the reader "watched". Broad strokes about what the demo thread builds are fine ("the ticket-desk code grows a queue of tuples"). No answer-key file path in prose (no walkthrough name, trainer path or demo-repo commit appears in prose either).
13. `window.DIAGRAM` present: `captions.length == step count`; every node has `step, x, y, w, h, label`; edges reference existing node ids; each step reveals something that matters for that section (not decoration); the last caption summarises what the reader can now explain.
14. `window.QUIZ.questions.length == metrics.notes.quizQuestions`; type mix per `quizTypeMix` (≥1 `scq`, ≥1 `tf`, ≥1 `mcq` or `match`, ≤1 `open`); `answer` indices in range; every `explain` present; questions test the page's content (which traces to the brief).
15. `window.NEXT = { label, href }` points to the next day's page (or `../../index.html` after the last teaching day of the week).
16. No code popup: no `button.code-cta`, no `#code-*` modal, no `.cv[data-file]` (D-0055). A runnable example lives in the step's own `<pre><code>` block.
17. Widgets allowed: `.ix-legend`, `.ix-predict`, `.ix-spot` only. Runnable blocks (D-0042, pilot): a `<pre>` in the article may carry `data-run="python"` when its `<code>` is a complete Python script that needs no terminal, no typed input and no file on disk, plus `data-continues` when it uses names from the runnable block directly above it. Never on a shell, git or output-only block. The page adds no script tag for this (item 9 stands: `widgets.js` does the work). Practice tasks (D-0043, D-0047): one `<section class="practice" id="practice" hidden>` between the last teaching step and Check yourself, holding a `.tag`, an `h2` and no prose of its own (it is not a `section.step`: item 5 counts and the diagram captions ignore it), may hold `div.ix-task` blocks. The notes author never writes this section: it is generated after the page is authored, from `harness/practice/<week>/<day>.json` by `python harness/practice_tasks.py insert`, and judged by `harness/critics/practice.md`. A page without it passes this item; a page whose author wrote one by hand does not. Each has `data-id` (unique on the page), `data-title` (the section it practises), `data-q` (the task: answerable from this page alone, nothing a later day teaches, no delivery reference) and two `<pre>` children, `task-starter` and a hidden `task-tests` whose top-level asserts each carry a message a reader can act on. The starter must not pass and the trainer's model answer must (`tests/test_assets.py`).
18. Prose reads like a person wrote it (D-0029; the `humanizer` skill's pattern list is the reference). FAIL on any of these in `.reading-col` prose, quiz `explain` text, interview callouts, check answers or diagram captions: "not X but Y" / "not just X, it's Y" contrasts; one-line closers that restate the paragraph ("That is the point."); forced triads where the meaning does not ask for three; em-dashes used as the default joiner (more than ~2 per section); bold labels at the start of list items or paragraphs (the `.k` label of a callout or a recap and a table's header row are structure, not bold labels; a recap item is a plain sentence fragment); stock AI words (delve, crucial, robust, seamless, leverage, landscape, tapestry, navigate, journey, foster, testament, underscore, vibrant). Quote the offending sentence and give the plain rewrite. Facts, code, inline code, paths and link targets are out of scope for this item.
19. The notes-verify findings passed in your prompt are binding: every `structural` entry (the page does not parse, counts outside the bands), every `dead` link and every `stale` claim is a critique unless you show it is wrong, and any of them means `pass=false`.
20. Assumed knowledge (D-0048, the Audience line): nothing tells the trainee they already know something, met it in coursework or university, or will recall it (`metrics.notes.assumptionPhrases` is the mechanical floor, not the whole rule), and every idea the page uses is either taught on this page or an earlier day of the course, or gets a one-line explanation where it is used. Assume only general programming ideas from any language (a variable, a branch, a loop, a function); Python syntax, Git, the terminal and CS theory are taught, never presumed. The `.summary` names what the page assumes from earlier days of this course only.
21. Exit criteria (D-0063; HARD from curriculum.json exitCriteria.fromWeek; the prompt lists the day's rows as EXIT CRITERIA). (a) Every criterion mapped to this day is answerable from the page: the step that teaches it (the plan's Notes step, realigned by the run's Wire step from the author's exitStepMap; do not fail on the number) covers it to the item-12b depth. (b) Every Required row is answered in substance by a .callout.interview <p> or a details.check answer; quote the criterion and the sentence that answers it, or name the gap. (c) The #goals-modal items are written from the day's Required criteria (each item names the substance of one or more rows, nothing invented, the item-8 band holds); a day whose rows carry no Required one writes them from the subtopics. (d) .lede .meta carries <span class="exit"><a href="../../exit-criteria/<group page>#<day id>">Exit criteria</a></span> and the target file holds that anchor. A day with no rows passes (a)–(c) vacuously and still needs (d). Before fromWeek the item does not apply.
