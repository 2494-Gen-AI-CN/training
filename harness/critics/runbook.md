# Runbook critic — PASS criteria

You are judging `trainer/<week>/<day>/runbook.md`. You are NOT the author. Return `pass=false` with specific critiques if ANY item fails.

1. Headings present, in this order: `# <id> runbook`, `## Day at a glance`, `## Objectives`, `## Setup check`, then the teaching blocks in the order the day runs them: one `## Demo: <walkthrough>` per walkthrough block of the day (`<walkthrough>` exactly as the week plan's ladder names it plus `.md`, e.g. `01-desk-modules.md`, the file in `trainer/<week>/demos/`; AM before PM) and one `## Facilitation N: <subtopic> (M min)` per concept subtopic that has no demo (on a `demoFormat: none` day every block is a Facilitation block and there is no Demo block); on a `demoFormat: notebook` day (D-0085) there is no Demo block and ONE `## Notebook: <file>.ipynb` section takes its place (item 3c), with Facilitation blocks only for the subtopics the notebook does not cover, `## Notes page handoff`, `## Sprint / project work`, `## Common questions`, `## If running long / short`. On `feedback: true` days also `## Feedback form`; on `kind: review` days also `## Review agenda`. A trailing `## Delivery record (<date>)` section is appended by `harness/close_day.py` after the day (D-0050, D-0055); it is allowed and never judged.
1b. Line 3 links every artifact as markdown links (D-0027, D-0055): the notes page on the public site (`config.siteUrl`), the week's walkthrough folder relatively (`../demos/`), and the research brief on the public repo (`config.repoUrl`, absolute, since the trainer repo is separate). Paths match the day's `rel` exactly.
2. "Day at a glance" is a markdown table with a Minutes column; the minutes sum to `metrics.runbook.dayMinutesMin..dayMinutesMax` (330–390). Breaks and lunch are included (a break inside a walkthrough is named in its row, not a row of its own), and one row is `Sprint / project work`. When the prompt carries week-plan rows (D-0034): each walkthrough row's minutes equal the plan's ladder minutes for that block, and the AM/PM order follows the plan's day shape.
3. Each `## Demo:` section is a pointer, not a second copy of the walkthrough: the walkthrough path (`../demos/NN-<slug>.md`, relative to the runbook), its Time and slot, its commit message on the prep branch (the plan's block row), the room state it needs before it starts (the previous block it starts from and what is on screen and in the repo, or "cold"), the stop point if the day runs long (a step number matching the plan's Stop points row, and where the remainder resumes), ≥2 checkpoint questions for the room, and ≥1 pitfall drawn from the research brief. The talk track lives in the walkthrough; a runbook that re-states the steps fails.
3c. Notebook day (D-0085): the `## Notebook: <file>.ipynb` section names the notebook's path in the demo repo (`notebooks/week-WW/NN_<weekday>_<slug>.ipynb` on `main`), how the trainer opens it in Google Colab and runs the setup cell first, then a timed pass through the notebook's `##` sections in order, one `### <section> (M min)` each (the headings as the notebook has them; while the notebook is not written yet, the sections the subtopics imply plus one line saying the notebook is authored after the research brief), each opening with a "Teach:" line (D-0089, runbooks authored from 2026-11-18: the idea the section introduces in 2–4 sentences, the term defined plainly and why it works), then 2–4 lines to say, one "Predict before running:" question with its answer the "Try changing:" prompt with what the room should see, and (D-0092, runbooks authored from 2026-11-18) a "Trap:" line and one or two "If asked:" lines (the likeliest hard question with its answer, sourced from the brief or the notes page; the full list lives in `crib-sheet.md`); it closes with the Recap and the Quick check questions to ask aloud and a stop point if the day runs long. It never copies the notebook's code cell by cell. The glance table's notebook rows sum to the section minutes.
3b. Each `## Facilitation N: <subtopic> (M min)` section is the presenter's lecture notes for that subtopic (D-0089), not an activity plan. The test: a trainer who has opened neither the brief nor the notes page can teach the subtopic from this section alone. In order: `**Goal:**` (what the room can do after); `**Topics introduced, in order:**` (the 2–6 ideas or terms new to the room in this block); one `### Topic k: <idea> (M min)` per listed topic, in that order, each with `**Teach:**` (3–6 points the trainer can say as written: the term defined in one plain sentence, the problem it solves or why it exists, how it works, and one concrete example, number or date from the brief), `**On screen:**` (the literal text, table rows or code the trainer types or pastes on the shared screen, written so it can be pasted; D-0090), `**Ask:**` (≥1 question with the answer the trainer steers toward and the wrong answer to expect) and `**Bridge:**` (one sentence into the next topic; the last topic bridges to the activity or the next block); at most one `### Activity: <what> (M min)`, placed after the topic it practises, with the task as said, its materials written out in full, the answer key and the debrief; then `**Misconception to surface:**` (≥1 from the brief, and how to surface it) and `**What the room writes down:**` (the lines themselves). Across the block ≥2 Ask questions. FAIL any line that names an activity or a topic without its content: "draw a timeline and fill it as you talk", "discuss the history", "ask the first two questions below", "explain how X works", an On screen line that describes a picture instead of giving the text to paste, a Teach point that says what to cover without saying it. Topic and Activity minutes sum to the heading's minutes, which match the glance table. Runbooks delivered before 2026-11-18 keep the earlier shape (Goal, Sequence, Ask the room) and are not re-judged.
4. Objectives (3–5) match the notes page `#goals-modal` list in substance. From exitCriteria.fromWeek (D-0063) the Objectives section ends with a table "Exit criteria mapped to today" (ID | Tier | Criterion | Where) equal to the prompt's exit rows, Required rows first; absent when the prompt carries no rows.
5. `## Sprint / project work` (D-0068) names what the room works on (the current sprint or official project spec on the site's Projects section), where trainees hand in: `<week-dir>/hackathon/` for a sprint, or for an official project the hand-in folder its spec names and never the week's hackathon folder (D-0094), in their own `<FirstnameLastname>` repository (D-0051, quoted in the prompt as LEARNER REPO), never a new per-day or personal-account repository, how the trainer circulates, and ≥2 likely stuck points. Its minutes match the glance table.
6. Every fact or version stated traces to the research brief, or to the day's notes page (which is written from the brief). Lecture notes (item 3b, the Teach lines of 3c) add no fact from outside the two.
7. Tone: direct instructions to the trainer, present tense, no filler.
8. Remote delivery (D-0090, runbooks authored from 2026-11-18): the course is delivered over a video call with a shared screen. Nothing in the runbook needs a physical room: no board or whiteboard, no printed cards or handouts, no walking the room, no show of hands, no "the pair beside you". What a board would hold is typed or pasted on the shared screen (a scratch text cell or file, named in the Setup check); a show of hands is a reaction or a chat answer; a lecture block (Facilitation, Notebook, Demo, Recap, wrap-up) has no pair work and no breakout room (D-0093): an activity is solo work whose answers go in the chat, sent together on the trainer's word; circulating is asking a learner to share their screen. Learners working on their own paper is fine.
