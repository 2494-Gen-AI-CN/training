Topic Title: One-Line Hook

Week 0 · Day 0 Mon Jan 1, 2026 Phase label (week theme) ~35 min · reference notes @150 wpm · diagram · check Sources checked 2026-01-01 · python 3.15

Three to five sentences. The first names, in one sentence, what this page assumes from the previous day. The next say what the page teaches and what the reader can answer afterwards. No sentence points back at a classroom moment: the page stands on its own as documentation.

Framing

Section heading that states the idea

Each teaching step is 450–900 prose words and teaches one or two subtopics to junior-interview depth: a plain explanation, a runnable example with its output stated, a trade-off or limit, how to recognise the thing in someone else's code, and the words to say when asked. Facts come only from the brief; a sentence that states a version, an API name's behaviour, a default or a limit cites its source like this.1

First subtopic as an h3

Explain the mechanism in plain words a graduate can follow. Single tokens go inline as python -m pytest -q; anything the reader would type goes in a fenced block, one command per line, no prompt characters, and the prose says what the output is.2

python --version
python -c "import sys; print(sys.version_info[:2])"

The second command prints (3, 12). Say what to notice and why it matters when the version differs.

The adjacent concept

The follow-up an interviewer asks next, from the brief's Notes depth. One level beyond the subtopic, never a later day's topic as a section. A later day's topic may be named in one sentence that says where it lands.

Say it in an interview

One to three spoken-form answers, first person, no code, each sayable in ten to twenty seconds, each answering one of this step's interview questions from the brief.

Mechanics

How it actually works

Prose that walks the mechanism. The diagram on the right reveals one more piece per step.2

A runnable example

git switch -c feature

State the output of each command and what changed on disk.3

Recognise it on sight

What the pattern looks like in a codebase you did not write: the one- or two-line shape, the name people use for it, the review comment it usually draws.

Say it in an interview

A spoken answer to "how does this work under the hood?" in two or three sentences.

Pitfall

Where people go wrong

Prose drawn from the brief's Common pitfalls, with the trade-off that explains why the wrong way is tempting.1

The mistake

What goes wrong, what the symptom looks like, and the fix.4

The trade-off behind it

for item in items:
    print(item)

When the index really is needed, and what to reach for then.

Say it in an interview

A spoken answer to "what is the common mistake here and how do you avoid it?"

Design choice

Choosing between the options

A subtopic that is a decision rather than a mechanism: the options, the cost of each, the one a team usually picks and why.5

Option A

print("option a")

Prints option a. What it buys and what it costs.

Option B

print("option b")

Prints option b. When this one wins.5

Say it in an interview

A spoken answer to "when would you choose A over B?"
