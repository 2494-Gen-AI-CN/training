### Handling missing values, outliers
- Interview questions: How do you handle missing values, and why that method? (qc4.ai-ml-fundamentals.R35); How do you decide whether an extreme value is an error or real? (qc4.ai-ml-fundamentals.R35); When does a missing-value indicator help?
- Adjacent concept: missing not at random — a blank that appears mostly for defaulters carries information, which is why the indicator column can beat a plain fill.
- Trade-off or limit: clipping or logging matters for linear models and networks, whose weighted sums feel a large value's size; a decision tree splits one column at a threshold (`x_j <= t_m`) and "requires little data preparation", and a log is monotonic, so it keeps the rank and leaves the tree the same possible splits — [Decision Trees 1.6](https://scikit-learn.org/1.6/modules/tree.html), [Preprocessing data 1.6, non-linear transformation](https://scikit-learn.org/1.6/modules/preprocessing.html#non-linear-transformation)

## Glossary terms (3–8; one-sentence plain-language definitions; aka = alternate surface forms)

- feature engineering | Building new input columns from existing ones, such as a monthly payment from a loan amount and its duration, and keeping each one only if it helps on validation rows. | aka: engineered feature, derived feature
- ordinal encoding | Turning a category with a natural order into the numbers 0, 1, 2 and so on in that order, so a model can use the order. | aka: OrdinalEncoder, ordinal encoder
- imputation | Filling a blank value with an estimate learned from the training rows, such as the column's median. | aka: impute, imputer, SimpleImputer
- outlier | A value far from most of a column's values, often defined as more than 1.5 interquartile ranges beyond the middle half of the data. | aka: outliers, IQR rule, 1.5 IQR rule
- F1 score | The harmonic mean of precision and recall for one class, which is high only when both are high. | aka: F1, f1-score, F-measure, macro F1, weighted F1
- decision threshold | The probability above which a classifier answers "yes", 0.5 by default, and best set from what each kind of mistake costs. | aka: classification threshold, cut-off, cutoff
- regularization | A penalty on large weights added to the training loss so the model stays simpler and overfits less; L1 sets some weights to zero, L2 shrinks them all. | aka: regularisation, L1 regularization, L2 regularization, weight decay, kernel_regularizer
- cross-validation | Splitting the training rows into k folds, training k times with a different fold held out each time, and reporting the mean and spread of the k scores. | aka: k-fold cross-validation, stratified k-fold, StratifiedKFold, cross_val_score

## Common pitfalls (3–6; feed the runbook "where trainees get stuck" and quiz distractors)

- Trusting the category order the data stores: credit-g's `employment` categories come back alphabetical (`'1<=X<4', '4<=X<7', '<1', '>=7', 'unemployed'`), so `OrdinalEncoder()` with no `categories` encodes "unemployed" as the highest; `categories=[ORDERED["employment"]]` must be a list of lists, and `checking_status` and `savings_status` stay one-hot because "no checking" and "no known savings" fit no order.
- Learning anything from rows other than `X_tr`: the median fill, the IQR fences, the frequency shares, the mutual-information ranking and the threshold all come from training or validation rows as the plan says; picking the threshold or the features on `X_test`, or carrying `### 5.4`'s threshold over to `best_pipe`, makes the final number optimistic.
