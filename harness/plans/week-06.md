## Weekly Epic

By Friday afternoon a learner can open a notebook from the demo repo in Google Colab, save a copy to their own Drive and run it top to bottom, then change it and explain what changed. In that notebook the learner loads a built-in dataset, splits it with `train_test_split`, fits a `LinearRegression` and reads its slope, intercept, mean squared error and R², shows underfitting and overfitting by changing a polynomial degree or a tree's `max_depth`, and scores a `LogisticRegression` classifier with `accuracy_score`. The learner clusters unlabelled points with `KMeans`, chooses K from the elbow and the silhouette score, scales features with `StandardScaler` before any distance-based method, and says when DBSCAN fits better. The learner explains a neural network's layers by running a forward pass in numpy, shows how a convolution and a pooling step read an image, and turns ticket titles into TF-IDF vectors that a classifier labels. In TensorFlow 2 the learner makes tensors with `tf.constant` and `tf.Variable`, reduces a tensor of any shape along an axis, turns a Python function into a graph with `tf.function` and shows it in TensorBoard, trains a perceptron by hand, then builds, fits and evaluates a one-layer Keras model and prints its summary. The artifacts are the learner's own Colab copies of the five notebooks, with their "Try changing" edits. Project 1 runs weeks 6 to 8 alongside TensorFlow (week 7) and feature engineering and model evaluation (week 8); its spec is not published yet; the load, split, fit and score loop and a one-layer Keras model are what its model work starts from.

## Real-world framing and deliberate deviations

On a data science or machine-learning team, a notebook is where a question is explored: an analyst pulls a sample from the warehouse (week 4's BigQuery), tries a few models in Colab, Vertex AI Workbench or JupyterLab, and shows the result to the team; once a model is worth keeping, its code moves into a reviewed Python package with tests, its data preparation into a pipeline, and its runs into an experiment tracker, and it is scored on the metrics the business agreed. The week's notebooks are the exploring half of that work, run on built-in datasets so every result can be seen and checked. A professional team would train on its own data, compare models with cross-validation and several metrics, pin its library versions in a managed environment, and review notebook changes in a pull request.
