## Weekly Epic

By Friday, after three teaching days that end Wednesday afternoon, a learner can open each of the week's notebooks from the demo repo in Google Colab, save a copy to their own Drive, run it top to bottom and explain every result it prints. In those copies the learner computes a mean squared error and a cross-entropy by hand and says which one a problem needs, walks gradient descent down a one-weight loss curve and shows what a too-large learning rate does, shows why one perceptron fails on XOR and one hidden layer solves it, and explains backpropagation on a two-weight network with numbers. The learner trains a Keras multi-layer perceptron on MNIST with a validation split, reads the training and validation curves for overfitting in a plot and in TensorBoard, and evaluates it with a confusion matrix, precision and recall for one digit, and the reason accuracy misleads on an unbalanced problem. The learner explains collaborative filtering and the restricted Boltzmann machine that recommends from a ratings table, trains an autoencoder that rebuilds digits from 32 numbers, composes an encoder and a decoder into one model, and writes the same network with the Sequential API and with the Functional API, including a skip connection Sequential cannot express. The learner adds batch normalization, saves a model to a `.keras` file and loads it back with the same predictions, writes the training loop by hand with `tf.GradientTape`, names dropout and regularisation as defences against overfitting, and compares two runs in TensorBoard. The artifacts are the learner's own Colab copies of the three notebooks with their "Try changing" edits. Project 1 runs weeks 6 to 8 and its spec is not published yet; the train, validate, evaluate and save loop of a Keras model is the model work it will need.

## Real-world framing and deliberate deviations

A model worth keeping moves into a reviewed Python package with tests, trains in a scheduled job on the team's own data with a pinned environment, saves its weights to a model registry, and is served behind an endpoint. Recommenders at a shop or a streaming service use matrix factorisation or deep recommenders over millions of ratings; autoencoders flag unusual transactions or sensor readings by their reconstruction error.
